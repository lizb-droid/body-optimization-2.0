### Pre-Race Sleep Protocol

**Tonight is the race-critical sleep night — not tomorrow night.**

Sleep two nights before competition governs reaction time, neuromuscular output, and emotional regulation on race day. The night before a race is nearly always compressed by pre-race adrenaline and early alarms — it matters far less than most athletes assume.

**Tonight, Jul 27 (Sunday — rest day, protected):**
- Phone down at 9:00 PM (not 9:30 — earlier than usual)
- In bed by 9:15–9:20 PM
- Sleep stack taken by 8:30 PM so it's at peak plasma level at onset
- Room at 67°F or cooler — pre-race anticipation will already be warming your core
- Target: 7.5 hours minimum (asleep by 9:30 → 5:00 AM wake)

**Tomorrow, Jul 28 (night before Brighton):**
- Keep the same bedtime as tonight. Consistency of timing > chasing extra minutes.
- Do not force sleep if you wake at 2–3 AM with pre-race mind activity — that's normal. No phone. Controlled exhale breathing (4-count in, 6-count out) brings sleep pressure back within 10–15 min.

### Phone Interventions (concrete, not aspirational)

**"Put the phone down earlier" is a goal, not an intervention. These are:**

1. **Move the charger to the kitchen — tonight, permanently.** This removes the phone from the bedroom entirely. Get a $12 alarm clock. The behavioral science: when the phone is in the bedroom, "one more check" is a one-step behavior. In another room it becomes a deliberate choice with a friction cost. That cost alone is enough for most people.

2. **Scheduled grayscale mode at 9:00 PM.** If the phone must stay in the bedroom during transition: iOS Screen Time → Accessibility Shortcut → Color Filters → set to activate automatically at 9 PM. Color-stripped screens remove dopaminergic stimulation — scrolling becomes genuinely less engaging because the brain's reward circuits respond specifically to color saturation. Pair with Night Shift at maximum warmth. This is a partial solution; relocation is the stronger intervention.
